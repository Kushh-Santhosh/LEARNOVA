Business Strategy: Porter Five Forces & Competitive Advantage

1. Industry Structure and Competitive Forces
Michael Porter identifies five fundamental competitive forces that shape industry attractiveness:
1. Threat of New Entrants: Barriers to entry such as economies of scale, capital requirements, and switching costs.
2. Bargaining Power of Buyers: When customers have numerous substitutes or buy in large volumes, they depress pricing.
3. Bargaining Power of Suppliers: Suppliers command power when inputs are differentiated or switching suppliers is cost-prohibitive.
4. Threat of Substitute Products: Substitutes place a price ceiling on industry products.
5. Rivalry Among Existing Competitors: High fixed costs and slow industry growth intensify price wars.

2. Generic Competitive Strategies
Firms establish superior profitability through either Cost Leadership (operational efficiency) or Differentiation (unique value proposition).
